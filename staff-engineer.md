[Job Description](job-descriptions/staff-engineer)

As a Staff Engineer, you lead through technical expertise, cross-team collaboration, and technical influence.
You shape architectural direction and mentor others, helping teams deliver scalable solutions.

## Impact

- Step into teams as a "traveling craftsman" to provide focused technical support, whether by accelerating delivery, addressing deep technical challenges, or mentoring team members.
  Spread effective agent practices (how to specify, delegate, and verify work) as you go.
  Transition out once the team is unblocked and self-sufficient.
- Leave behind clear, valuable artifacts such as documentation, diagrams, utilities, design patterns, rules files, or skills that continue to support the team (and its coding agents) after you've moved on.
- Identify patterns and common needs across multiple teams, and proactively create scalable tools, frameworks, automations, or guidance that help solve shared problems efficiently.
- Set and drive engineering standards that elevate code quality, reliability, and maintainability across your operating group.
  Make them enforceable automatically (through linters, tests, evals, and pipeline gates), not just documented, so they bind agents and humans alike.
- Build shared agent infrastructure for your operating group: guardrails, evaluations (evals, automated checks of the behavior and output quality of artificial intelligence, or AI, tools), and the context agents need, such as Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers.

## Reach

- Operating Group

Your work consistently affects teams across your operating group, informing technical decisions and standards broadly.
You lead through influence and expertise, not just authority.

## Knowledge

- Demonstrate deep expertise in core technologies and architectural patterns used across the organization.
- Shape architectural decisions with a long-term, strategic mindset, optimizing for scalability, maintainability, and business alignment.
- Draw on a strong foundation in engineering principles to take on unfamiliar technologies with confidence.
  Adapt and apply core concepts to new domains, enabling effective problem-solving across varied tech stacks.
- Understand how agents fail: where they hallucinate, over-reach, or follow injected instructions, and which permission boundaries limit the damage.
  Use that understanding to set standards that scale review rather than overwhelm it.
- Maintain a strong understanding of engineering workflows, standards, architectural decisions, and the broader system landscape to provide relevant context, promote alignment, and guide decision-making across teams.

## Communication

- Write and present proposals that influence technical direction across multiple teams.
- Facilitate architectural reviews and technical deep dives with empathy and clarity.
- Coach others in how to document, diagram, and communicate their own thinking effectively.
- Translate abstract or technical risk into understandable trade-offs.
- Drive conversations toward clarity by reducing complexity and identifying actionable next steps.
- Advocate strongly for your position in cross-team decisions, then commit fully once a direction is set, and help the teams you support do the same.
- Partner with the Engineering Managers in your operating group, your counterparts on the Management Track, to align technical direction with delivery and team health.

## Leadership

- Remain a hands-on engineer throughout, including reading and reviewing code with a skeptical eye; verification is the bottleneck, not typing.
- Model engineering excellence and act as a multiplier, raising the bar across your operating group through mentorship, coaching, and influence.
- Model intellectual humility, curiosity, and high standards; the culture you carry shapes the culture of the teams you touch.
- Keep the learning path open: when agents absorb the small tasks, help teams create deliberate ways for junior engineers to build real understanding.
- Hold teams accountable for what they ship, whoever or whatever wrote it.
- Facilitate learning across teams through initiatives such as architecture guilds, postmortems, or engineering summits.
- Own and improve engineering processes, standards, and tools that support a healthy, inclusive, and effective engineering culture.
- Maintain a bias to action across the teams you support: keep work moving, surface and resolve blockers, and build momentum without creating dependency.
- Raise the hiring bar: interview candidates, calibrate technical assessments across teams, and help new senior engineers ramp up quickly.
