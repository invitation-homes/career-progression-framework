As an Engineering Manager, you are a technical and people leader who enables your team's success.
You make sure your engineers are growing, your work processes are effective, your systems are healthy, and your team is delivering meaningful results.
You are accountable for execution, collaboration, and team culture.
Your pod is humans and agents (artificial intelligence, or AI, tools that write code and run multi-step tasks), and you own what the whole system ships.
Think of yourself as a player-coach: equally invested in the technical work and in the people, culture, and conditions that let your team thrive.

## Impact

- Cultivate teams through relationships, culture, recruiting, and growth within your pod.
- Use domain knowledge to plan and drive delivery of solutions aligned with business goals.
- Facilitate the team's execution by identifying and removing obstacles and by improving processes, collaboration, and prioritization.
- Own the health of your team's systems: advocate for reliability, maintainability, and automation alongside delivery.
- Run your pod as a system of humans and agents: own its outcomes, quality, and cost, and decide where agents take work and where humans stay in the loop.
- Keep verification ahead of generation: review capacity, not typing speed, limits delivery, so push for smaller batches and stronger automated gates.
- Evaluate technical risk, including the risks agents add (security, resident data, permissions, cost), and help your team make sound decisions even when the path forward isn't clear.

## Reach

- Leadership Reach
  - Team

- Technical Reach
  - Operating Group

While your leadership focus is your team, your technical influence extends across your operating group: you collaborate with other teams, product partners, and stakeholders to align on vision and coordinate delivery.
You share knowledge, influence culture, and support consistency across the broader engineering organization.

## Knowledge

- Maintain a deep understanding of your team's systems and the technical stack they use.
  You are capable of diving deep to help unblock, troubleshoot, or guide.
- Anticipate future technical and organizational challenges, and steer the team toward scalable and sustainable approaches.
- Maintain a deep understanding of the tools and processes across both engineering and management practices, including what agents do well, where they fail, and how to verify their work.
- Understand how your team's work connects to company strategy, resident experience, and business metrics.

## Communication

- Communicate clearly, proactively, and transparently with your team, stakeholders, and partners.
- Translate technical concepts for non-technical audiences and vice versa.
- Provide thoughtful feedback that promotes alignment and action.
- Own what your pod ships. "The agent wrote it" is never an explanation for an incident or a defect.
- Resolve ambiguity and conflict constructively and encourage healthy team discussions and decision-making.
- Speak up when you disagree, commit fully once a decision is made, and build the same norm on your team.
- Partner with the Staff Engineers who support your team, your counterparts on the Professional Track, to align technical direction with delivery and team growth.

## Leadership

- Cultivate a high-performing, inclusive, and psychologically safe team environment.
- Coach, mentor, and guide engineers as they grow in their careers, including how they work with agents.
  Watch for overreliance and deskilling, and make sure junior engineers understand and can explain what they submit.
- Keep the junior pipeline healthy: as agents absorb small tasks, create deliberate learning opportunities for early-career engineers.
- Manage performance with clarity and care: address gaps early, before they become problems, and invest in growth.
- Lead hiring end-to-end: build strong pipelines, assess judgment with AI tools allowed, represent Invitation Homes well to candidates, and champion a great candidate and new hire experience.
- Delegate effectively, trust your team, and hold them accountable for their commitments.
- Judge people by outcomes, quality, and judgment, never by AI usage or output volume.
- Model the values and behaviors you want to see across the organization: curiosity, humility, a bias to action, and genuine care for others.
