[Job Description](job-descriptions/director-of-engineering) · [Being a Director of Engineering](being-a/being-a-director-of-engineering)

As a Director of Engineering, you shape the strategic technical vision, align engineering execution with business goals, and build an environment where managers and teams thrive.
Your focus is on driving architecture direction, managing organizational risk, scaling technical systems, encouraging innovation, and delivering high-impact results.
You influence not just engineering, but the broader organization's culture, priorities, and success.

## Impact

- Deliver strategic impact by shaping technical strategy and executing initiatives that advance organizational priorities.
- Surface and manage organizational risks: systemic gaps, architectural drift, and capability shortfalls that span teams or threaten delivery.
- Establish an organization-wide culture of engineering excellence.
- Guide vendor relationships and system integrations so technology partnerships deliver high value and appropriate risk assurance.
- Establish and maintain technical governance across your operating group: standards, decision frameworks, and accountability structures that outlast any individual initiative.
  Standards that agents consume (rules files, skills, Model Context Protocol servers) and automated gates enforce them, so they hold without a human reminder.
- Manage organizational spend in alignment with strategic goals, including AI (artificial intelligence) spend such as tokens, which you treat as a budget.
- Set your operating group's AI risk posture: policy for agent permissions, resident data handling, auditability, and change control (CAB, Change Approval Board) for agent-initiated changes.
- Own the evaluation strategy for AI-assisted work in your operating group, so you measure quality by outcomes and evals (automated checks of AI behavior and output quality) rather than by volume.

## Reach

- Leadership Reach
  - Operating Group

- Technical Reach
  - Department

You lead an operating group, and your technical influence extends across the engineering department.
You partner closely with leadership across Product, Design, Business Operations, and Vendor Management so technology and strategy are deeply integrated.

## Knowledge

- Maintain technical expertise, evaluate system and organizational trade-offs, and apply emerging trends to drive scalable innovation.
- Understand relationships and internal dynamics to align engineering initiatives with strategic priorities.
- Maintain a strong understanding of business functions and their roles in driving outcomes.
- Understand principles of organizational design to structure teams effectively for growth, scalability, and delivery.
- Understand how agents change delivery: verification increasingly limits throughput, and the risks of autonomy (prompt injection, over-broad permissions, unreviewed changes) grow with every workflow you delegate.
- Understand the financial dimensions of engineering: budget management, headcount planning, and aligning organizational spend with strategic priorities.

## Communication

- Create alignment through transparent, strategic communication, connecting technical decisions to business outcomes.
- Communicate priorities, trade-offs, progress, risks, and results across the organization.
- Build and maintain trust with executive and cross-functional leadership through consistent, well-reasoned communication.
- Represent engineering's perspective in company-level conversations, helping the broader organization understand and value the work of your teams.
- Speak up when you disagree, and once a decision is made, commit fully and create alignment behind it across your operating group.
- Partner with Principal Engineers, your counterparts on the Professional Track, to set technical strategy and governance for your operating group, including platform direction for agents.
- Own the outcome when AI-assisted work fails: "the agent wrote it" is never an explanation to executives, residents, or associates.

## Leadership

- Build a generative, high-performing culture where individuals and teams have the autonomy and support to innovate and succeed.
- Guide, coach, and inspire engineering managers, growing the next generation of technical and organizational leaders.
- Build and nurture cross-functional collaboration.
- Maintain accountability across the organization.
- Champion automation and operational efficiency as organizational priorities: the pods that scale best eliminate manual toil before it accumulates, including by delegating to agents with verification built in.
- Maintain a bias to action at the operating group level: anticipate organizational risks before they surface and keep engineering moving with clarity and momentum.
- Model strategic thinking, empathy, and continuous improvement at every level of engagement.
- Serve as a steady decision-maker and mediator, providing clarity, resolving conflicts, and enabling teams to move forward with confidence and focus.
- Own talent strategy for your operating group: hiring plans, recruiting partnerships, and the pipeline of future Engineering Managers.
  Hire for judgment, with AI tools allowed, and keep the junior pipeline healthy even as agents absorb the small tasks that once trained new engineers.
- Coach your EMs to run pods of humans and agents: build AI fluency, watch for deskilling and overreliance, and never measure people by AI usage or output volume.
