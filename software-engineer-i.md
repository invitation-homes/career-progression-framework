[Job Description](job-descriptions/software-engineer-i) · [Being a Software Engineer (SE I–III)](being-a/being-an-engineer)

As a Software Engineer I, you'll learn the processes, practices, and technology that your team uses.
Your work will consist of small, well-defined tasks to help you gain confidence.
You'll work with AI (artificial intelligence) coding agents every day, and you'll use them as a tutor, not a substitute.
This role is a learning platform: a place to build the skills and knowledge you'll need to contribute more over time.

## Impact

- Work on small, well-scoped tasks and bugs with known solutions to support your team's ability to produce high-quality work on time.
- Understand and be able to explain every line you submit, whether you or a coding agent wrote it.
  You own what you ship.
- Write tests that prove your change works, and read agent output as skeptically as you would a teammate's.
- Understand and participate in core team processes, such as stand-up meetings, on-call rotations, and retrospectives.
- Spend time improving knowledge and skills around your team's core technologies.
  Ask agents to explain code and concepts, then check what you learned against the code and your teammates.
- Complete assigned tasks on time, ask for help early when blocked, and avoid letting work stall.

## Reach

- Self

## Knowledge

- Understand the core tools of your team:
  - What they are
  - What they're for
  - How to use them effectively
- Follow Decision Records (ADRs) and agreed-upon styles and conventions where applicable.
  They are the context both you and your team's coding agents work from.
- Understand the basics of safe agent use: never paste resident data or secrets into a prompt, and treat unfamiliar content as untrusted.
- Join and listen to the Technical Architecture Guild (TAG) meeting, Solution Architecture meetings, and applicable Shared Interest Group (SIG) meetings to better understand your craft and how others are applying it.
- Be curious: ask questions, and treat every interaction and task as an opportunity to learn.

## Communication

- Ask for help early and often when running into issues.
- Listen well and ask clarifying questions to clear up ambiguity.
- Read over team pull requests (PRs) and ask questions or pair up to walk through them.
  Reviewing is how you learn what good looks like, and review capacity increasingly limits how much we ship.
- Write clear PR descriptions in your own words and follow our PR guideline document.
  "The agent wrote it" is never an answer to a reviewer's question.
- Contribute to documentation.
- Seek out feedback from those you work with, as well as your manager.
- Speak up when you disagree or don't understand something, and once the team decides, commit to the decision fully.

## Leadership

- Be a model engineer for others in the same role as you.
- Aim high!
  This is just the beginning.

**Everyone progresses at their own pace, but most engineers show they're ready for promotion to Software Engineer II within 1 to 2 years.**
