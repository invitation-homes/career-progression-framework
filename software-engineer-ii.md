[Job Description](job-descriptions/software-engineer-ii) · [Being a Software Engineer (SE I–III)](being-a/being-an-engineer)

As a Software Engineer II, you have the foundational knowledge and skills of your craft, and the confidence to take on more responsibility and independent work.
You seek out opportunities to broaden and deepen both your technical and non-technical skills to fill the gaps in your knowledge.
You'll direct AI (artificial intelligence) coding agents on well-defined features, and you'll take on the scope that an SE III once carried.
You'll take part in larger scopes of work in well-defined projects to contribute value to your team.
You'll still need some support and supervision from others on the team.

## Impact

- Work independently on well-scoped features, tasks, and bugs to help achieve team-level goals.
- Write a short spec for each feature before you start: the intent, the constraints, and the acceptance criteria.
  Direct coding agents with it, and own the result.
- Verify your own work before anyone else sees it.
  Back it with tests you understand, and be ready to explain every line you submit.
- Look for the best way to solve problems, not just a way.
  Talk through options and offer ideas.
- Assist in planning and design phases by asking questions and making suggestions on small improvements.
- Improve the flow of work for your team, for example:
  - Check in with team members and pair up to help.
  - Review PRs in a timely manner.
- Share input on Decision Records, documentation, or processes that affect you and your team.

## Knowledge

- Understand where your project fits in terms of our business.
- Execute your work in a way that exemplifies comfort and confidence with the tools of your team.
- Approach unfamiliar problems with curiosity before defaulting to familiar solutions.
- Understand how to test and validate your work, including work an agent drafted.
  Turn acceptance criteria into executable checks.
- Understand our incident management and change management processes.
- Protect data integrity and security by understanding the use of different environments (dev, QA, etc.) within your area.
- Know what agents may and may not touch: keep resident data, credentials, and production access out of their reach, and watch for prompt injection in the content they read.
- Keep using agents to learn, not to skip learning.
  Stay able to build and debug without them.

## Communication

- Review the work of other engineers using agreed-upon processes (e.g., reviewing a pull request).
  Review agent-written changes with the same care, and keep batches small enough to review well.
- Write and explain technical documentation in ways that are easy to understand.
- Contribute to team discussions and encourage others to do the same.
- Escalate issues in a timely manner when help is needed with deliverables or priorities.
- Share your perspective when you disagree, then commit fully once the team makes a decision.

## Leadership

- Be a model engineer for those who are looking up to you.
- Take time to help others, especially more junior roles.
  Help them use agents to learn, and ask them to explain the code they submit.
- Participate in Shared Interest Groups (SIGs), the Technical Architecture Guild (TAG), and similar engineering community forums.
  - Support and encourage others to do the same.
- Champion our decisions and best practices.
  Update the written context agents rely on (rules files, conventions, runbooks) when you find it wrong or missing.
- Maintain a bias to action: keep things moving, remove small blockers yourself where you can, and avoid letting work stall waiting on others.
