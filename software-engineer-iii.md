[Job Description](job-descriptions/software-engineer-iii) · [Being a Software Engineer (SE I–III)](being-a/being-an-engineer)

As a Software Engineer III, you are capable of leading the design and implementation of a typical solution.
You aren't expected to have all the answers, but you have the aptitude and experience to solve the problems in front of you effectively.
You decompose multi-step work and orchestrate AI (artificial intelligence) coding agents across it.
Your work is high quality: you deliver what is needed in a timely manner and with excellent communication to your peers, management, and stakeholders.
You share what you have learned with your peers, juniors, and our engineering community.

## Impact

- Lead technical implementations of projects to help your team deliver high-quality work on time.
- Support your team by breaking down and coordinating work, splitting it into pieces that people and agents can deliver and verify in small batches.
- Own the design and the evals (automated checks of AI behavior and output quality) for your features.
  Know how you will tell good output from plausible output before you start.
- Proactively identify new opportunities and implement improvements to current products.
- Work independently to drive code reviews and engineering designs to completion.
- Proactively identify and remove obstacles that slow down your team's ability to deliver.
- Look for opportunities to eliminate repetitive manual work through automation, even in small ways.
  Delegating to an agent counts when you verify the result.

## Reach

- Team

## Knowledge

- Understand the implications of work being done and adjust your approach based on impact and risk.
- Understand the team and organizational structure in our engineering department and how to work and communicate within it.
- Draw on the depth and breadth of your knowledge of good engineering practices and non-functional requirements to solve problems at a more complex or abstract level.
- Keep up to date with best practices and recent technology changes in your domain.
- Use data and metrics to validate assumptions and inform the decisions you make about your work.
- Understand the risks of agent autonomy in your area: permissions, resident data, prompt injection, and the cost of the tokens you spend.
  Scale your guardrails to the stakes.

## Communication

- Gather input and ask clarifying questions to make informed and timely decisions.
- Highlight any potential pitfalls or decisions that have complex technical consequences early.
  Help make sure we're building the right thing, not just building it right.
- Create diagrams and documentation for your team's products: write things down so the whole team can move independently.
  Humans and agents both work from what you write, so a gap in context hurts both.
- Share what you've learned in Shared Interest Groups (SIGs), the Technical Architecture Guild (TAG), or our Monthly Learnings.
- Set up postmortems and identify actions to avoid repeating problems.
- Lead structured meetings that run to time and produce tangible outputs.
- Voice disagreement clearly and early.
  Once a decision is made, commit to it fully.

## Leadership

- Mentor more junior engineers where possible, such as helping find suitable tasks, pairing with them on tickets, or providing context and guidance.
  Agents now do much of the small work that used to teach people, so create learning opportunities on purpose.
- Review the work of others and of agents, and coach your pod on using agents well: clear specs, small batches, and honest verification.
- Help others on your team progress by inviting them to pair up on work that may be beneficial for them to learn.
- Assist with onboarding, such as being a culture coach for a new team member in their first month.
- Be involved with our engineering community by:
  - Bringing up important issues
  - Encouraging others
- Ask "why" even in challenging situations, and recognize and call out assumptions and risks.
- Begin to shape team culture by modeling the behaviors and standards you want to see: quality, curiosity, and accountability.
- Begin participating in the hiring process by joining interview panels and providing feedback on candidates.
- Maintain a bias to action: keep work moving, model momentum for your team, and help address blockers before they stall delivery.
